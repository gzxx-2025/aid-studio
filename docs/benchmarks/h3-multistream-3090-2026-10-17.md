# MiniMax H3 second-round acceleration benchmark

Date: 2026-10-17. Host: `gpu-222`, two RTX 3090 24 GiB cards, no NVLink, GPUs on separate `SYS` PCIe/NUMA paths. Both cards had their normal 350 W limit; the primary link negotiated PCIe 3.0 ×8 and the secondary PCIe 3.0 ×16. Production workers were drained through the GPU scheduler before testing. The benchmark used an isolated ComfyUI service on loopback port 8193 and did not modify the production workflow.

## Method

- Fixed prompt, seed `20261017`, H.264 plus model-generated audio.
- Standard model: `minimax_h3_fl2va_pruned_int8_convrot.safetensors`.
- Fast model: `fastvideo_fasth3_8step_v2_pruned_int8_convrot.safetensors`.
- MultiStream: host exchange, eight exchange chunks, DynamicVRAM `keep`, pinned weight cache.
- `H3BenchmarkForceRun` invalidated the sampler graph for every run while preserving model and text-encoder residency.
- The same-seed dense single/dual outputs were decoded and compared by frame hash and audio frame MD5.

## Findings

### MultiStream dense

- Warm 480P end-to-end time improved from 55.062 to 45.051 seconds: **18.2% faster**.
- Sampling improved from about 8.0 to 6.5–6.7 seconds per step, approximately **19%**.
- The decoded video frames and decoded audio were bit-exact between the single- and dual-GPU dense runs.
- Each dual 480P step moved about 10.77 GiB through host exchange. The separate `SYS` paths and lack of NVLink prevent near-linear scaling.
- Each dual 768P step moved about 31.53 GiB; 8–10 seconds of an 18–20-second step were host exchange. The primary card's PCIe 3.0 ×8 link is a material ceiling.
- MultiStream is therefore a validated lossless accelerator for exclusive dual-GPU quality jobs, but not automatically the best farm-throughput policy: one clip occupies both GPUs.

### Sol-Attn

Logs confirmed the CUDA Sol-Attn path on all 50 blocks, with no dense fallback. Sampling became slightly faster, but VAE decode and memory retention made total time 80.068 seconds, slower than dense. Side-by-side frames showed duplicated people, smearing and ghosting. The tested `tau=1.3`, start `0.2` configuration is rejected.

### FastH3 VSA

VSA executed on all eligible blocks. Against the dual FastH3 dense arm, warm end-to-end time improved 11.8% (85.072 to 75.067 seconds). It was nevertheless slower than the existing single-GPU FastH3 production baseline of about 46.9 seconds. The VSA output also introduced unwanted Chinese subtitles and changed composition despite the fixed seed and `no text` instruction. It is not a production default.

## Recommendation

1. Keep the current single-GPU FastH3 preview worker as the default fast path.
2. Retain `h3-dual-dense` as an opt-in, scheduler-exclusive quality mode only after the 768P arm is accepted.
3. Do not enable the tested Sol-Attn or VSA settings in production.
4. Next lossless candidate: cache conditioning and AV latents in separate phases so repeated seeds do not reload/re-run the 25 GiB text encoder.
5. Next cold-start candidates: test the existing W6A8 DiT and the smaller NVFP4/AWQ text encoder independently. On Ampere, NVFP4 is emulated, so treat it as a memory/staging optimization rather than assumed compute acceleration.
6. Test FirstBlockCache only as an approximate preview mode with multiple prompts and an editorial pass-rate gate.

## 768P validation

The model/shape-switch run completed in 1035.687 seconds (17 minutes 16 seconds), compared with the earlier single-GPU cold result of 1931.030 seconds. The warm repeat completed in 430.341 seconds (7 minutes 10 seconds), compared with the earlier production single-GPU warm result of 1409.896 seconds. This is a 69.5% operational wall-time reduction, but it combines MultiStream, retained model weights and reusable text conditioning; it is not a pure tensor-parallel speedup.

Both runs produced valid H.264/AAC media at 1344×768, 24 fps and 5.166 seconds. Their decoded video-frame hashes and decoded audio-frame hashes were identical. Five evenly spaced review frames showed coherent identity, room geometry and motion with no obvious duplicate subject, subtitle, or gaze defect.

If both cards are reserved continuously for this profile, the warm theoretical rate is about `86400 / 430.341 × 5 = 1004` generated 768P seconds/day. One model-switch run followed by warm repeats yields about 995 seconds/day. A safer planning range is 850–900 generated seconds/day before editorial rejection. This capacity consumes both GPUs and therefore displaces the independent FastH3 preview lane.

## Third round: lossless phase cache and native quantization

The third round kept the same isolated port, scheduler-exclusive dual-GPU
profile, prompt, dimensions and 20-step sampler. The cache implementation was
pinned to `pepikir/minimax-h3-speedup` commit
`4081f500ed733eebcdb5853cb0a75b9aa5e14c9f`. It stores text conditioning and
sampled AV latents separately so a repeated seed can skip text encoding and
decode can be retried without diffusion.

| Job | DiT | Text encoder | Encode | Warm sample | Warm decode | Cached repeat | Full repeat | Decision |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 31 | INT8 ConvRot | INT8 ConvRot | 270.338 s | 140.233 s | 24.064 s | 164.297 s | 502.737 s | cache accepted |
| 32 | W6A8 | INT8 ConvRot | 2.014 s* | 146.208 s | 24.053 s | 170.261 s | 506.796 s | not default |
| 33 | INT8 ConvRot | NVFP4/AWQ heretic | 192.259 s | 142.280 s | 24.046 s | 166.326 s | skipped | candidate |

`*` Job 32 reused already-resident conditioning and is not a valid cold text-
encoder comparison.

For Job 31, the cached repeat reduced end-to-end render work by **67.3%**
(`502.737 → 164.297 s`, 3.06×) and the cached and full paths had identical
decoded video and audio hashes. This validates lossless reuse for the exact
text-to-video conditioning tuple tested. It does not validate reference-image
reuse; cache invalidation must include prompt, reference hashes, dimensions,
frames, model/encoder/VAE/LoRA identities and conditioning parameters.

W6A8 reduced the pinned DiT cache from 18.04 to 14.11 GiB and its cold cache
build from 227.5 to 167.0 seconds, but its warm sampler was 4.3% slower than
INT8. Its output was internally cache-equivalent, yet differed materially from
INT8 at the same seed (average PSNR 13.831, SSIM 0.648), including composition
and background changes. It is retained only as a memory/cold-staging option.

The NVFP4/AWQ text encoder staged 14.955 GB instead of 25.882 GB (42.2%
smaller) and reduced cold encoding by 28.9% (`270.338 → 192.259 s`). Sampling
was unchanged within noise. Its output was closer to the INT8 encoder than the
W6A8 DiT comparison (average PSNR 20.795, SSIM 0.780) and showed no obvious
corruption in five sampled frames, but it is not numerically equivalent and
needs multi-prompt plus reference-image review before routing production work.

### Third-round disposition

1. Promote phase caching only as an opt-in repeated-variant accelerator for an
   exact validated T2V conditioning tuple; do not make it a general cache.
2. Keep INT8 ConvRot DiT as the quality default.
3. Retain NVFP4/AWQ text encoding as the next cold-start candidate, not the
   default.
4. Do not combine approximate block caches or sparse attention with this
   lossless result until each has its own quality arm.
5. Production FastH3 was restored through scheduler Job 34 after the lab was
   released. Its cold validation completed in 675.767 seconds and produced a
   valid 4-second 480P clip; GPU1 returned to `WARM h3-single-fast`, GPU0 was
   free, and the dual experimental profile was disabled again.

## Fourth round: ordered reference cache and FirstBlockCache

Jobs 35–36 extended the phase cache to the production-style Ref2VA graph with
two ordered reference images. At 832×480, 107 frames and four Turbo-LoRA steps,
the cached repeat took 82.871 seconds versus 366.684 seconds through the full
graph: a **77.4% reduction (4.42×)**. Decoded video and audio frame hashes were
identical. A second semantic check used two visibly distinct assets—an adult
man in a dark suit and an adult woman in a floral blouse—and confirmed that
both identities, costumes and input order appeared correctly in the scene.

Reference order is part of the conditioning contract. A production cache key
must therefore cover the ordered file-hash list, prompt, dimensions, frames,
checkpoint, encoder, VAE, LoRA and all conditioning parameters. Any mismatch
must be a hard cache miss.

Jobs 37–38 tested the MIT-licensed FirstBlockCache commit
`d474755360cc1784df1b7c933ca28195e55e3445` in the isolated runtime, using the
same two references, seed and 20-step `res_multistep` sampler.

| Arm | Job 37 sample | Job 38 sample | Warm repeat result | Quality against dense |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Dense | 112.173 s | 112.168 s | baseline stable | reference |
| Safe | 162.243 s* | 118.186 s | 5.4% slower | SSIM 0.8893, PSNR 25.94 dB |
| Fast | 82.137 s | 90.131 s | 19.6% faster | SSIM 0.8923, PSNR 26.11 dB |

`*` The first Safe run included its first compiler-path overhead and is not a
warm comparison. Fast cached 9/20 steps and Safe cached 7/20. Each arm's
decoded output reproduced exactly between Jobs 37 and 38, and both references
remained recognizable. Safe is rejected for throughput. Fast is retained only
as an approximate, opt-in 20-step preview candidate; do not combine it with a
Turbo/low-step adapter or another block cache, and do not make it the final-
quality default before a multi-prompt face, gaze, hand, motion and audio pass.

Raylight/xDiT was rejected at source-review stage rather than deployed. Its
published two-RTX-3090 INT8 result is 36.0 seconds/step at 1344×768 and uses a
separate PyTorch 2.8 runtime; the validated local MultiStream stack is already
about 6.6 seconds/step warm at that resolution. Deploying the slower alternate
runtime would add operational risk without a plausible throughput gain.

After the fourth round, the dual profile was disabled and released. Scheduler
Job 39 cold-started the production FastH3 worker on GPU1 and generated a valid
four-second 480P clip in 804.864 seconds. Final state: GPU1 `WARM
h3-single-fast`, GPU0 `FREE`, no running or queued jobs, and the experimental
dual profile hidden/disabled.
